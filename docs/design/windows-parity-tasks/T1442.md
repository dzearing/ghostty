# T1442 - The agent's scrollback-bearing attach repaint is never measured against a real deep-scrollback session

## In plain terms

T621 made a re-attached pane come back with its history already in hand instead
of waiting for the whole recent output to be re-sent. Every check that it works
was written against a handful of lines. Nobody has yet watched it happen to a
pane holding a real day's worth of output, which is the only place the wait would
ever have been noticeable.

## Goals

- [ ] the snapshot's size at the retention ceiling is measured against the ring
      replay it replaces
- [ ] the agent's real per-session memory at that ceiling is measured against
      T621's stated ~3 MB
- [ ] if the trade does not hold at real sizes, the ceiling is re-chosen from the
      measurement

## Summary

T621's coverage is correctness, not magnitude. The unit tests use 12-20 lines
through a 3-5 row screen: enough to prove the scrollback is retained, reflowed,
ordered, gated by the capability and absent on a delta attach - and nothing about
what the change is FOR, which is the size of the payload and the wait.

Two numbers are unmeasured. What a scrollback-bearing repaint actually costs at
the 1 MiB retention ceiling (`grid_snapshot.max_scrollback_bytes`), serialized as
VT with styles: it could plausibly exceed the 2 MB ring it replaces for heavily
styled content, which would invert the whole trade without anything going red.
And the agent's real resident cost per session at that ceiling, which T621
STATED (~3 MB with the ring) but did not weigh.

## Details

Shape: an acceptance arm - or a bench in the agent lane, which is cheaper and
does not need a GUI - that fills a session's emulator to the retention ceiling
with representative output (a build log, a `claude` transcript: wrapped lines,
SGR colour, some alt-screen churn), then measures:

- the serialized snapshot size at `scrollback = true`, against the ring size the
  attach would otherwise have sent, at several attach geometries;
- peak RSS of the agent with N such sessions, against the stated ~3 MB/session;
- the wall-clock from ATTACH to the client having parsed it.

The number to defend is that the repaint is smaller than the ring replay it
replaces at the sizes users actually reach. If it is not, the fix is a smaller
`max_scrollback_bytes` (or a row cap on serialization) chosen from that
measurement rather than from the estimate T621 made.

Related: `test\win32\pane-ingest-lag.ps1` already knows how to measure a pane's
ingest cost and may be the right place to hang the client half.

## Validation criteria

- [ ] a bench or acceptance arm fills the emulator to the ceiling with
      representative styled output and reports snapshot bytes vs ring bytes
- [ ] the comparison is made at a DIFFERENT attach geometry than the capture
      geometry, since reflow is the reason the payload is worth sending at all
- [ ] agent RSS per session at the ceiling is reported against the stated ~3 MB
- [ ] if the repaint is not smaller than the replay it replaces, the retention
      ceiling is re-chosen from the measurement and T621's stated budget updated
- [ ] floor: all four lanes green
