# T646 - Viewer feedback: thumbnail carousel under the composer, scroll-synced with the chips

## In plain terms

When you paste pictures into the feedback box you get an `[Image #1]` tag in
your text, but no way to see what you actually attached. On a Mac a row of
thumbnails sits under the box, and clicking a tag scrolls to its picture (and
the other way round). Windows needs that row too.

## Summary

Split out of T637, which landed the image half of the composer: storage, stable
chip numbering, clipboard paste, and the images written into the report. What
this adds is the SURFACE — a thumbnail carousel below the pill that mirrors the
chips, and two-way scroll sync between a chip and its thumbnail.

Mac site: the carousel half of `ViewerFeedbackComposer.swift`.

## Details

- The carousel derives from the same storage the chips do
  (`viewer_feedback_images.Store` filtered by `live()` against the composer
  text), so a deleted chip's thumbnail disappears without any second
  bookkeeping path — the derive-from-storage rule.
- **Chip numbers are stable, not positional**, and T637 already guarantees that;
  the carousel must show `#1, #3` after `#2` is deleted, never renumber.
- Geometry belongs in `viewer_feedback_layout.zig` (a third row below the pill
  and above the footer, EMPTY when there are no images so no band of blank
  pixels is left behind), asserted at 1.0/1.25/1.5/2.0 like the rest of it.
- Thumbnails are decoded and scaled with the existing `gdiplus_decode.zig`
  (`decodeScaled` takes an `IStream` — a stored PNG goes in through
  `CreateStreamOnHGlobal`), cached per image so a repaint is not a decode.
- **Clicking a chip scrolls to its thumbnail and vice versa.** The chip side has
  a pure span finder already (`viewer_feedback_images.chipAt`); the thumbnail
  side needs a hit test and an `EM_EXSETSEL`/`EM_SCROLLCARET` on the matching
  chip.

## Goals

- [x] A carousel row under the pill, sized and placed by the pure geometry
      module, that costs nothing when there are no images.
- [x] Thumbnails decoded once and cached, letterboxed rather than stretched.
- [x] Click a thumbnail → its chip is selected in the composer.
- [x] Caret in a chip → its thumbnail is ringed and scrolled into view.

## Validation criteria

- [x] Unit tests (none lane): the carousel row's geometry at every scale, empty
      when there are no images and with no gap left behind.
      *Verified: `viewer_feedback_layout.zig` — "no images means no carousel row
      and no gap left behind", "the carousel is a row of square tiles between
      the pill and the footer", "the strip scrolls only when it has to, and
      never past its ends", "a tile only answers a click on the part of it that
      is on screen", "fitInto letterboxes rather than crops"; all loop
      1.0/1.25/1.5/2.0. Teeth-checked by breaking the footer-offset assertion
      and watching it go red.*
- [x] Unit tests (none lane): the carousel's contents derive from live chips —
      1,2,3 minus #2 shows 1,3 in that order.
      *Verified: `viewer_feedback_images.zig` — "the carousel shows the live
      chips, in strip order, with the hole kept".*
- [x] On-box acceptance: paste two images, both thumbnails appear; delete the
      first chip and its thumbnail goes with it.
      *Verified: `test\win32\viewer-feedback-carousel.ps1` arms B and D — and
      "appear" is the decode, not just the count: the app reports each tile it
      actually rasterised (`thumb=#N box=B decoded=WxH`) and the script checks
      the aspect ratio it came out at.*
- [x] On-box acceptance: clicking a thumbnail selects its chip in the composer.
      *Verified: same script, arm C — the click is answered with the chip's
      character range, and one Backspace over it removes the whole chip, which
      only a real selection does.*
- [x] Standing floor green: `scripts\floor-lane.ps1 -Lane all`, plus P1–P3 and
      the two sibling composer scripts (`viewer-feedback.ps1`,
      `viewer-feedback-images.ps1`).
