# T1228 - Sweep the acceptance harnesses for fixed sleeps used as handshakes, the shape behind the section-M flake

## Summary

Found while doing T1164. `test\win32\suite-run.ps1`'s section-M fixture raised a
dialog from a detached process, slept a flat `Start-Sleep -Seconds 3`, and
exited - and the sweep it was testing runs AFTER the script exits, so on a box
slow enough that WinForms had not finished painting the sweep found nothing and
M1 through M4 all went red at once with no defect behind them. Measured on this
box: 0.23s to paint idle, and the task was filed during the soak work, which is
exactly the load. T1164 replaced the sleep with a bounded wait on the sweep's
own predicate and proved the difference with an 8-second-delayed dialog (old
shape `verdict=pass modals=0`, new shape `verdict=fail modals=1`).

The shape, not the instance, is the subject here: a fixed sleep standing in for
"wait until X is true" is a race whose failure mode is a cluster of unrelated-
looking FAILs on a busy box, and there are ~290 scripts in `test\win32\`.

## Details

Enumerate `Start-Sleep` in `test\win32\` (and the fixtures the harnesses write)
and sort each into one of three buckets: a real settle (a debounce the product
itself has, where the sleep IS the subject), a poll interval inside an
until-loop (fine), or a handshake - "by now the other thing must have happened"
- which is the bug. Convert the third bucket to bounded waits on the predicate
the assertion actually cares about, with the timeout producing a legible
failure that names what never happened, the way T1164's M5 now does.

## Validation criteria

- [ ] every `Start-Sleep` in `test\win32\` is classified, and the handshake ones
      are listed in this task with their file and line
- [ ] each converted site waits on a predicate and fails with what never
      happened, not with a downstream assertion
- [ ] the harnesses touched are green, and their guard rows re-stamped
