# T239 - Chevron glyph reads ~30% lighter than the + and x at the same stroke width

## Summary

Surfaced while landing T232. The banner's collapse chevron is built from two
arms whose thickness is measured **vertically** (`b.bottom - t` .. `b.bottom`
at the outer end), while the "+" and the hamburger are built from bars whose
thickness is measured **perpendicular** to the mark, and the "x" was explicitly
corrected to perpendicular in T232 (design system §4.3).

The chevron's arms are at 45° (`mark_chevron_w` 12, `mark_chevron_h` 6 → 6
across and 6 of rise per half), so a vertical thickness of `stroke_w` paints a
perpendicular thickness of `stroke_w / sqrt2` ≈ **0.71 × stroke_w**. Three of
the four chrome glyphs land at `t`; the chevron lands at `0.71t`. That is the
same class of defect as the hamburger's optical width — one control quietly
disagreeing with the set — and the design system's own §4.2/§4.3 already say it
is a defect.

`? INFERRED` on the visual severity: this is arithmetic off the source, not a
user report, and 0.71t on a 2 DIP stroke is a sub-pixel difference at 100%. It
is most visible at 1.5x and above, where `stroke_w` is 4 px and the chevron
paints ~3.

## The fix

Either give the chevron a perpendicular-corrected vertical thickness
(`tv ≈ 1.4 * stroke_w`, which needs `mark_chevron_h` to grow so the rise is
not eaten by the thickness), or make the arms genuinely shallower than 45° —
which is what "shallower than a caret" in the design system was asking for
anyway — so vertical thickness and perpendicular thickness converge. Prefer
the second: it fixes the weight AND makes the glyph match its own spec.

## Validation

- A unit test in `icon_button.zig` asserting every glyph's PERPENDICULAR mark
  thickness lands within 1 px of `stroke_w`, at every scale 1.0–3.0 — i.e. the
  `close`-only assertion T232 added, generalised to the whole set.
- `test/win32/pane-banner.ps1` ALL PASS (it already covers the chevron's box
  and hover), plus an ASCII dump of the chevron off a real capture at 125%
  showing arms the same weight as the "+" beside it in the strip.
