# T941 - The tab strip and caption probes still take the torn photo - convert the main-window chrome scripts to the synchronous capture

## Summary

T843's second half for the main `Window` class, unblocked by T940's
`WM_PRINTCLIENT` handler. Switch every `Get-TestWindowPixels` call that
photographs the top-level app window to `-Sync`, and re-run each script several
times to confirm the flake is gone rather than moved.

Scripts, from the T843 survey: `tab-strip.ps1`, `tab-color.ps1`,
`caption-bar.ps1`, `chrome-theme.ps1` (the `$g.Top`/`$h` sites),
`chrome-merged-row.ps1`, `close-glyph-white.ps1`, `hero-mode.ps1`,
`hero-viewer-tile.ps1`, `hover-capture.ps1`, `menu-bar.ps1` (the `$g.Top`
site), `remote-pill.ps1`, `split-divider.ps1`, `title-font.ps1`,
`title-jitter.ps1`, `window-color.ps1`, `readonly-badge.ps1`, and the shared
`lib/ChromeGeometry.ps1` helper.

`lib/ChromeGeometry.ps1` is the leverage: it captures on behalf of several of
those scripts, so converting it converts them together — do it first and see
what is left.

## In plain terms

Most of the tests that check how the window's chrome looks are working from a
photograph that can come out half-taken. This points them at the steady one.

## What the flake actually measured

The number this task was written to move is smaller than the one T835 measured,
and the difference is worth recording so T942/T943 are not sold a promise the
evidence does not support. Two purpose-built probes on 2026-08-19, both against
the debug build at 1100x700 on the background test desktop:

- **Back-to-back captures of a steady window.** 20 default captures and 20 sync
  captures, 64 sample points each over the caption + strip band: **0 of 20
  disagreed in either mode.** The main window is not layered, so DWM hands back
  its redirection surface without the tearing T835 measured on the layered
  `BannerOverlay` (three captures of one unchanged row at 1062 / 1283 / 1179 px).
- **A capture taken right after a change.** The window title was driven between
  a short and a long string with `+rename`, then photographed after a fixed
  settle and scored against per-mode references taken after 2 s. At a 150 ms
  settle and at a **0 ms** settle: **0 of 20 and 0 of 30 frames wrong** in
  either mode — every frame showed the new title, none the old, none a mixture.

So for the main window the before-rate is 0%, not the "one run in three" of the
banner. What `-Sync` buys here is therefore not a flake that was firing daily;
it is (a) the removal of a dependency on DWM having finished compositing, which
is timing-dependent by construction and was never going to be provable safe by
sampling, and (b) a capture that FAILS on a window that drew nothing instead of
returning a blank frame that a "no ink" assertion passes against.

One behavioral difference the conversion does carry, measured rather than
assumed: the two captures of the same steady window are byte-identical over the
whole CLIENT area, and differ only in the non-client frame — roughly 8 columns
each side and 6 rows at the bottom, which the default capture renders black and
the sync capture renders in the real border color. Any probe that reads the
frame itself (rather than client pixels) changes meaning when converted;
`caption-bar.ps1`'s maximized scan already started at the client top, and its
comment now says why that matters under `-Sync` too.

## Notes for the sibling conversions

`chrome-theme.ps1`'s four `$panel` sites are `ActivityMonitor` captures that
T943 also claims. They were converted here rather than left behind, because the
script is one run and re-running it three times for two tasks proves nothing
twice. T943's remaining scope is `activity-monitor.ps1`, `confirm-dialogs.ps1`,
`config-errors.ps1`, `split-dim.ps1` and `split-dim-viewer.ps1`.

`readonly-badge.ps1` is named in the Summary but has no capture call at all —
only a comment about the limit. Nothing to convert.
