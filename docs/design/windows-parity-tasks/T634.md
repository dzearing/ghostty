# T634 - Viewer feedback: the composer toolbar chrome (pill, buttons, open/close, geometry)

## In plain terms

Pressing the feedback button should slide open a small message box under the
viewer's navigation bar — a rounded input with a button to attach a screenshot
and a button to send. This task builds that box and everything about how it
appears, sizes itself and goes away. Typing into it and sending it are the
next two tasks; what lands here is the thing you can see and open and close.

## Summary

Second of the T384 split. The composer's **chrome**: the toolbar strip below
the nav bar, the pill that grows with its content (one line up to ~6), the two
circular buttons inside the pill's trailing edge (`+` screenshot, `↑` send),
and the open/close lifecycle. No text model, no images, no report — those are
T635/T636/T637.

Mac sites: `ViewerFeedbackBar.swift` (the toolbar host and its layout) and the
chrome half of `ViewerFeedbackComposer.swift`; layout is pinned on Mac by
`ViewerFeedbackBarLayoutTests` / `ViewerFeedbackChromeLayoutTests`, and the
win32 equivalent is a pure geometry module with the same coverage.

## Details

Behavior to match:

- The feedback button **toggles** the composer. The nav bar **stays pinned
  open** while the composer is open — it cannot auto-hide out from under it.
- `Escape` closes the composer. `Ctrl+Enter` sends (the win32 spelling of
  Mac's `Cmd-Enter`); plain `Enter` inserts a newline. Register these with the
  pane-scoped accelerator table (`viewer_accel.zig`), under the same
  override-only-while-the-viewer-has-focus rule as Ctrl+R / Ctrl+D: a focused
  terminal keeps its own bindings.
- **Composer state lives on the pane, not on the toolbar** — Mac is explicit
  that contents survive toggling the toolbar closed and open again. On win32
  the natural mistake is to own the buffer in the child window and destroy it
  with the window; own it in `ViewerPane` and let the chrome render it.
- The pill **grows with its content** and stops at ~6 lines, after which it
  scrolls. The pane's WebView2 host and the terminal below reflow to the
  toolbar's height the way they already do for the nav bar.

Geometry belongs in a pure module (`viewer_feedback_layout.zig`, sibling of
`viewer_nav_layout.zig`) and is asserted at 1.0, 1.25, 1.5 and 2.0. The win32
design system applies in full: 4 DIP spacing scale, >= 4 DIP between painted
edges, one icon-button size with the standard rest/hover/pressed/disabled/
focused states, radius scale, 3:1 contrast for glyphs, filled-shape glyphs
(never `LineTo` strokes) for the `+` and `↑`.

Open question for the turn that takes this: whether the composer is its own
child HWND or is painted into the pane's existing chrome surface. Whichever it
is, the caret and IME must work (see T635's control decision) and the pill must
not steal focus from the page when it is merely open.

## Validation criteria

- [ ] Unit tests (none lane): composer layout at 1.0/1.25/1.5/2.0 — pill
      bounds, both circular buttons inside the trailing edge with >= 4 DIP
      clearances, height for 1..6+ lines, and the toolbar height the pane
      reflows to.
- [ ] On-box acceptance script: the feedback button opens and closes the
      composer; the nav bar stays visible while it is open; Escape closes it;
      the WebView2 content area shrinks and restores by the toolbar height.
- [ ] On-box acceptance: text typed into the composer is still there after
      closing and re-opening the toolbar (state lives on the pane).
- [ ] On-box acceptance: with a terminal pane focused, Ctrl+Enter and Escape
      do their normal terminal thing — the composer bindings are pane-scoped.
