# T381 - The viewer error card's PAINT has no on-box coverage: drive it with GHOZTTY_WEBVIEW2_BROWSER_DIR once a viewer is constructible

## Summary

T373 shipped the native error card — the thing a viewer pane paints when the
box has no WebView2 runtime — and its GEOMETRY is asserted at 1.0/1.25/1.5/2.0
in `viewer_error_card.zig`. Its PAINT has no coverage at all: this box has the
runtime installed, so every live test here takes the success path and the card
has never been rendered on screen.

## Details

The drill exists already and costs nothing: `GHOZTTY_WEBVIEW2_BROWSER_DIR`
overrides the probe outright (T372), so pointing it at a directory that does
not exist makes a box WITH the runtime take the runtime-absent branch
faithfully. What is missing is a viewer to point it at — nothing constructs one
from IPC until T374, which is why this is deferred rather than done here.

Worth measuring, not just screenshotting: the card is the first thing a user on
a WebView2-less box sees where content should be, so it has to clear the
design-system floors like any other surface — 4.5:1 for the message line and
3:1 for the rim against the card fill, on a light background as well as a dark
one (the card derives its own fill from the PANE background, so a light
terminal in a dark Windows theme is a real configuration to check).

*Validation:* `viewer-panes.ps1` grown with an error-card case — launch with
`GHOZTTY_WEBVIEW2_BROWSER_DIR` pointed at nowhere, open a `--view` pane, and
pixel-probe the card: it is present, centered, clears the pane edge by the
scaled margin, and its two text colors meet their contrast floors against the
measured card fill. Repeat with a light `background` in the config.

## In plain terms

A viewer pane needs Microsoft's WebView2 runtime to show a page. On a machine
that does not have it, the pane does not go blank - it draws a small card
saying what is missing and how to fix it. That card had never been on a screen
in any test: this box HAS the runtime, so every check we ran took the happy
path, and the only evidence the card existed was a piece of arithmetic agreeing
with itself. Now a test forces the runtime-missing case, takes a picture of the
pane, and measures the card: where it sits, and whether its two lines of text
are actually readable on the colour behind them - on a dark terminal and on a
light one.
