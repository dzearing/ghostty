# T209 - On-box pixel assertions for the tab strip's icon buttons and tab shape (blocked on T207)

## Summary

T204 (icon buttons) and T206 (tab silhouette + rim) shipped in `4565e4e42`
with their pure geometry and pixel math fully unit tested in both lanes, and
verified visually by a `PrintWindow` capture. What they did NOT get is the
on-box acceptance layer the standing bar asks for: `test/win32/tab-strip.ps1`
still holds its 18 T202 geometry assertions and nothing about the new
appearance.

The reason is deliberate and worth recording, because it is a scheduling fact
rather than an oversight. Mid-task the user said:

> "you KEEP STEALING FOCUS USE ANOTHER DESKTOP for testing"

So the GUI scripts stopped being run at all on this box, and the honest state
of T204/T206 is "unit-green, visually confirmed, not acceptance-tested".
Writing pixel assertions now would mean either running them on the user's
desktop (the thing they just asked to stop) or writing tests nobody can
execute. Both are worse than waiting for **T207** to land the isolated
desktop, which is exactly the infrastructure these assertions need.

## What to assert once T207 exists

Extend `test/win32/tab-strip.ps1` (it already has the harness, the launch
path, and a working negative control):

**T204 - icon buttons.** Flip `T204_NEUTERED` in
`src/apprt/win32/icon_button.zig` for the negative control; the assertions
below must fail with it set and pass with it clear, while the T202 geometry
ones must be unaffected either way.

1. The "+", "=" and close "x" glyph bounding boxes share a vertical center
   within 1 px, and each glyph is centered in its own target box. (The pure
   test pins the geometry; this pins that the PAINT actually uses it.)
2. Hovering the close button produces a rounded FILL, not only a color
   change - sample the pixel just inside the target's corner radius and
   assert it differs from the tab fill. Pre-T204 this is the assertion that
   fails, because the close button had no fill at all.
3. Hovering the banner chevron does the same. Needs `pane-banner.ps1` rather
   than `tab-strip.ps1`, and a mouse-move into the overlay window.
4. Click-through at the painted center of each of the four controls, proving
   paint and hit test still agree. This is the specific regression the change
   risks, and `chevronBox` being one definition is what should make it hold.

**T206 - tab shape.** Negative control is `T206_NEUTERED` in
`src/apprt/win32/tab_shape.zig`.

5. An INACTIVE tab is distinguishable from the strip background - sample a
   pixel in its middle and assert a measurable delta. Pre-T206 the delta is
   exactly zero, which is what "inactive tabs should be visible somewhat"
   meant.
6. A rim pixel along a tab's top edge is brighter than both the strip and the
   tab's own fill, and a rim pixel near the baseline is dimmer than the top
   one (the gradient, not just a border).
7. The gap between two adjacent tabs reads as strip background for
   `tab_gap` px.
8. The selected tab is WIDER at its baseline than at its middle - the flare.
   Sample a column just outside the tab's side at both heights.
9. Antialiasing: across the top-left corner arc there is at least one pixel
   that is neither strip nor fill. A GDI region produces none, so this is
   what separates the new path from the old one.

### The headline: BOTH negative controls were claiming more than they did

T209's own instructions assume the two source-level controls adjudicate the
assertions it asks for. Neither did, and finding that out is worth more than
any single assertion here.

- **`icon_button.T204_NEUTERED` moved no glyph at all.** `glyphCentered()` was
  exported, unit-tested, and consumed by NOBODY - both paint sites called
  `targetBox` directly. So the control changed the fills and left every glyph
  exactly where the shipped build puts it, and the centering assertions this
  task exists to write could not have failed no matter how the flag was set.
  Fixed with `icon_button.glyphTarget(m, box, glyph)`, which both sites now
  call. Its neuter branch aligns the MARK's leading edge to the box's - not the
  target square's, which was the first attempt and moved a glyph by only 3 px
  at 125%: indistinguishable from the +2 px measurement bias below, so the
  control "failed" by 1 px. Aligning the mark moves it 13 (measured dx=-24
  against a bound of 3).
- **`tab_shape.T206_NEUTERED` left the flare and the antialiasing on**, while
  its own doc comment claimed it removed the flare. Two of this task's four
  shape assertions therefore had no control either. Widened: `sdTabRim` drops
  the flares and `renderTab` takes a hard-edged coverage under the neuter, so
  the corner arc goes from 25 distinct values to exactly **2** - a GDI region,
  which is the pre-T206 world by definition.

The pattern in both: **a negative control that answers a question no paint
site asks is decoration.** Neither would have been caught by reading either
module - the giveaway is only visible from the call sites.

### What is asserted, and what the harness cannot reach

Deterministic, in `tab-strip.ps1`: the "+" and the active tab's close "x"
share a vertical center (measured centers, no derived coordinate enters it)
and each mark is centered on both axes of its own square; an inactive tab is a
visible surface and is distinguishable from the selected one; adjacent tabs
are separated by `tab_gap` of real strip; the top edge carries a rim brighter
than both fill and strip and that rim FADES down the tab; the selected tab
flares at its baseline; the top-left corner arc is antialiased. In
`pane-banner.ps1`: the chevron hot-tracks and un-hovers.

**The hover FILL is not assertable on this desktop, and the reason is now
measured rather than suspected.** T233 established that a posted
`WM_MOUSEMOVE` cannot HOLD a hover (`TrackMouseEvent` watches the real cursor,
and there is none). T209 found the sharper statement: the hovered frame is
never *painted at all*. `WM_MOUSELEAVE` is a POSTED message and `WM_PAINT` is
the lowest priority in the queue, so the leave is always drained first and the
paint that the move dirtied renders the un-hovered state. 300 posted moves in
bursts of 25 never once caught a lit fill - not on the close "x", not on the
"+" that has lit one since long before T204, not on the chevron.

So both scripts probe it with the "+" as the harness's own POSITIVE CONTROL
and SKIP when it is not caught: "+ caught, x not" would be a product defect,
"neither caught" is the race. The claim is carried meanwhile by three other
legs - the pure tests (`paintsFill`, `fillRegion`, `universalHover`), the
TRIGGER oracles added here (`tab hover ...` and `banner chevron hover=` debug
lines, the `hero hover tile=` idiom), and `caption-bar.ps1`'s pressed-fill
pixel probe, which runs the identical `paintIconButton` path with a state that
DOES survive the leave (`caption_pressed` is deliberately not cleared by
`WM_NCMOUSELEAVE`). **T282** is the follow-up: give the harness a held hover.

### Three test-authoring traps this turn walked into

1. **A tab's measured right edge reads ONE PIXEL SHORT, by construction** -
   the last fill column carries the side rim, and the rim is brighter than the
   "is this the chiclet?" threshold. That 1 px doubles into the centering
   delta, so the shipped build sits at dx=+2 and the bound is 3, stated rather
   than tuned.
2. **A scan outboard of a tab can reach the "+" button.** The flare scan's pad
   was `corner + 4`, which overruns `group_gap - btn_pad`; under the T204
   control (where the "+" mark moves 13 px left) it measured the button and
   turned a healthy flare red.
3. **`else` hid three of the four shape failures.** The inactive-surface claim
   and the silhouette claims are independent, and the silhouette ones only
   need the SELECTED tab - measurable in every world. Gating them behind
   "3 tabs are measurable" meant the T206 control could only ever show ONE
   assertion failing, which is the "green because it never got there" shape
   these assertions exist to prevent.

Also replaced two oracles that lie in opposite directions: a closed tab's pane
HWND survives the click by seconds (read 3 -> 3), and a painted-chiclet count
is blind under T206_NEUTERED (1 before AND after). `+list --json`'s tab array
is neither.
