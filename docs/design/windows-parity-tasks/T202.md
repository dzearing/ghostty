# T202 - Tab strip is not Windows-native: geometry, shape and selection idiom

## Summary

User-reported 2026-07-30 with a screenshot, single-tab window:

> "this looks super ugly ... there's some weird blue line ... why is the blue
> line on the bottom???? and then there's a + button which is butt up against
> the edge of the tab and looks clipped and a weird hamburger button which is
> misaligned from the x button. It just looks HORRIBLY amateur. Like a backend
> developer built the ui."

Four complaints, ONE structural cause plus three idiom errors. All four are in
`drawTabBar` in `src/apprt/win32/Window.zig`. The bar is a hand-painted GDI
strip that was grown feature-by-feature (T72 stripe, T78 font, T190 menu
button) and has never had a visual design pass against the platform it is
imitating.

This task is the geometry and shape half. Color/theming is **T203**, the
control glyphs are **T204**. Do this one FIRST - it establishes the layout
model the other two paint into.

## Root causes (verified by reading, line numbers at HEAD)

1. **A single tab is stretched to the full window width.** `Window.zig:2741` -
   the last tab is given `available_w - x` as its width, so with one tab it
   takes the entire strip. `max_tab_w` (200 DIP, line 2725) is computed and
   then bypassed by that rule. No Windows shell app does this: Terminal,
   Explorer, Edge and VS Code all size tabs to content between a min and a max
   and leave dead strip space to the right. This one bug produces THREE of the
   four visible symptoms - it is what pushes the close button to the far right
   edge, what makes the "+" appear to be jammed against a tab edge, and what
   stretches the accent line into a full-width rule.

2. **The selected-tab indicator is the wrong control's idiom, applied
   full-bleed.** `Window.zig:2766-2775` fills a 2 DIP accent bar across the
   ACTIVE tab's whole width at `bar_h - accent_h`. A bottom accent underline is
   the Fluent **NavigationView / Pivot** selection idiom, where it is a short
   bar centered under the item - it is not how a TabView marks selection. WinUI
   TabView marks the selected tab as a **rounded-top chiclet filled with the
   content-area background**, so the tab visually merges with the pane below
   it and needs no line at all. Stretched across a full-width tab, ours reads
   as a stray blue divider sitting under the strip, which is exactly what the
   user asked about.

3. **Zero gap between the tabs and the strip buttons, by construction.**
   `available_w = client_w - new_tab_btn_w - menu_btn_w` (line 2721) and the
   buttons are then drawn starting at `btns_left = client_w - new_tab_btn_w -
   menu_btn_w` (line 2857) - the same coordinate. The tab area ends on exactly
   the pixel the "+" begins, with no separation of any kind. Combined with (1)
   and with the glyph being left-aligned in its box (T204), that is the
   "clipped" look.

4. **The "+" is pinned to the right edge instead of following the last tab.**
   Lines 2856-2866 pin it to `client_w - new_tab_btn_w - menu_btn_w`. In WinUI
   TabView the AddTabButton immediately follows the last tab and travels with
   it; the right edge is the TabStripFooter, which is where the menu button
   belongs. Pinning both to the right is why they read as one undifferentiated
   cluster. NOTE: the pinning was deliberate (see the comment at 2850-2855 -
   past ~20 tabs, tabs overrun `available_w` and anything drawn after them
   lands off-screen). The fix is not to un-pin naively: tabs must stop growing
   at `max_tab_w` and start scrolling/compressing before they can overrun, so
   the "+" can follow them safely. Keep the overrun repaint as a backstop.

## Follow-up defect: no right-edge padding (user, 2026-07-30)

> "the hamburger button has no gap between it and the border"

Confirmed in the in-flight `tab_strip_layout.zig`: `Metrics` has
`strip_pad_l = px(4.0)` but **no right counterpart**, and `layout()` sets
`menu_left = client_w - m.btn_w` with `.right = client_w` - the menu button
ends exactly on the client edge while the first tab is inset by 4. Asymmetric
by omission.

Add `strip_pad_r` (the same 4 unless the box says otherwise) and subtract it
from `menu_left`. Note this interacts with **T205**: once the strip shares a
row with the caption buttons, the right inset becomes the gap to the caption
group, not to the window edge - so keep it a named metric rather than folding
it into the `client_w` arithmetic. Assert `menu.right + strip_pad_r ==
client_w` in the layout unit tests, and assert the left and right insets match
so the strip cannot drift asymmetric again.

If T202 has already landed when this is read, do it as part of **T204** (which
owns the button group) rather than reopening T202.

## Cohesion with the banner card (user directive, 2026-07-30)

> "the tab should have similar borders to the banner. It should feel cohesive"

Filed as **T206** (deps T202+T203) because this task was already in flight
when it came in. If the chiclet's shape work here can cheaply leave room for
it, do so: T206 gives the tab the banner card's specular rim (hairline,
alpha 0.28 top -> 0.04 bottom) and its 6%-on-dark / 4%-on-light fill wash,
via tokens extracted from `banner_card.zig`. Do NOT copy the card's 14 px
radius onto a 32 px tab - see T206 for why that and the drop shadow are the
two things that must NOT carry over.

## Design reference

Target is **WinUI 3 TabView as Windows Terminal uses it**, not a Mac port -
the stated goal for win32 chrome is that it look Windows-native.

Verify each of these on the box against live Windows Terminal / Explorer
rather than trusting the numbers here (they are from the WinUI defaults and
should be treated as a starting hypothesis, not ground truth):

- Tab sizing: content-sized between `TabViewItemMinWidth` (48) and
  `TabViewItemMaxWidth` (200), equal-shrink only once they would overflow.
  Never stretched to fill.
- Selected tab: rounded TOP corners only (~4-7 DIP), filled with the content
  background so it merges into the pane below. No underline.
- Unselected tab: transparent over the strip; hover is a subtle fill overlay
  (Fluent "Subtle Fill Secondary", roughly 5-6% white in dark).
- Strip height around 32-40 DIP at 100%.
- "+" immediately after the last tab; menu button in the footer at the right
  edge, with real separation between the two groups.

## Deliverables

1. A short written visual spec (in `docs/design/`, referenced from this task)
   with the measured Windows 11 values, so T203/T204 and future work paint to
   one agreed target instead of re-deriving it.
2. Tab width model: content-measured, clamped to [min, max], equal-shrink on
   overflow, no full-width stretch, no last-tab remainder rule.
3. Selected tab drawn as a rounded-top chiclet in the content background;
   delete the full-bleed accent underline. The T72 user tab-color stripe stays
   (it identifies the tab, not focus) but must be re-fitted to the new rounded
   shape rather than being a square band across a square tab.
4. "+" follows the last tab; menu button pinned right; a real gap between
   groups. Tabs must be unable to overrun the button band - keep the existing
   repaint as a backstop and add a test for the many-tab case.
5. Geometry extracted to a **pure module** (`tab_strip_layout.zig`) with unit
   tests, the way `banner_layout.zig`, `chooser_layout.zig` and
   `split_geometry.zig` already are. The current code computes layout inline
   inside a 300-line paint function, which is why none of this is testable
   today.

## Evidence (2026-07-30)

**Measured the reference first, rather than quoting WinUI defaults.** Live
Windows Terminal captured with `PrintWindow(PW_RENDERFULLCONTENT)` and read
back pixel by pixel at 120 DPI (125%). Three tabs took 900 px of a 1610 px
strip and left ~700 px empty — tabs are an equal share capped at a maximum,
never the remainder. The selected tab is a rounded-top chiclet (radius ~8 px =
6 DIP) filled with the content background `RGB(12,12,12)`, unselected tabs
have **no** fill at all, adjacent plain tabs get a 1 px `RGB(68,68,68)`
separator, and there is no accent underline anywhere in the strip. Full table
and the two deliberate deviations (32 DIP strip height, 200 DIP max width):
`docs/design/win32-tab-strip.md`.

**What changed.**

- `src/apprt/win32/tab_strip_layout.zig` (new, pure, 11 unit tests in the
  none lane). Equal-share width clamped to [60, 200] DIP with **no last-tab
  remainder rule**; tabs that will not fit at the minimum get a ZERO rect
  (invisible and unhittable) instead of being laid out under the button band;
  the "+" travels one `group_gap` behind the last tab up to a hard limit;
  the menu button is pinned to the right end of the strip, inset by
  `strip_pad_r`. Also owns `closeRect`/`titleRect`,
  `chicletRegion`, `buttonFillRegion` and `separatorRect`.
- `paintTabBar` now consumes that layout: selected tab = rounded-top chiclet
  in the content background (the accent underline is **deleted**), hover = the
  same shape in the hover fill, unselected = the strip shows through, T72
  color tag clipped to the chiclet so it takes the rounded corners, and a
  hairline between two adjacent plain tabs.
- The close box was recomputed from loose constants in **three** places
  (paint + click + hover); all three now call `Metrics.closeRect`, so paint
  and hit test cannot drift. Every tab hit test skips zero rects. The
  drag-reorder slot math no longer assumes tabs start at x = 0.
- **Right-inset defect from the live review folded in** (it arrived while this
  was in flight, so it did not need to go to T204): `strip_pad_r` = 4 DIP,
  the menu button is inset from the client edge by the same amount the first
  tab is inset on the left. Asserted in both the unit tests and the
  acceptance script.

**Validation, on the box.**

- `test/win32/tab-strip.ps1` (new, 18 assertions): a single tab is 250 px of
  a 1810 px client (== `max_tab_w` at 125%, not the window width); it starts
  at the 4 DIP inset; the dead strip right of it is bar background; **no
  accent-blue pixel exists anywhere in the bottom 3 rows of the strip**; a
  click in the gap after the last tab creates nothing; a click one gap further
  creates tab 2 and the "+" then MOVES right by exactly one tab width, where a
  second click creates tab 3; ~19 tabs shrink to 113 px and the run still stops
  short of the button band; a click in the right pad opens nothing while the
  menu button's centre still opens its popup. **ALL PASS**, run twice.
- **Negative control, run for real:** `T202_NEUTERED = true` in the layout
  module restores the last-tab remainder rule; rebuilt
  `-Dapp-runtime=win32` and re-ran. Exactly the geometry assertions failed
  (single tab 1695 px wide, width no longer clamped, the "+" pinned and both
  of its click assertions dead) — 6 failed / 11 passed. The accent-rule, dead
  space, many-tab band and menu assertions all still passed, so they are
  controls rather than filler. Restored to `false` and rebuilt.
- Regressions: `split-divider.ps1` ALL PASS (25); P1/P2/P3 each `ACCEPTANCE:
  ALL PASS`. `tab-color.ps1` updated for the new geometry (tab centres now
  include the 4 DIP inset and the two 36 DIP buttons + two 8 DIP gaps; the T72
  stripe sample moved down to the chiclet top).
- **`tab-color.ps1` and `window-title.ps1` fail on this box right now, and
  it is NOT this change.** Both die on keyboard-driven steps. A/B proved it:
  the pre-change sources were parked, `-Dapp-runtime=win32 -Doptimize=Debug`
  rebuilt from `HEAD`, and both scripts failed the same way (tab-color at its
  own positive control, `barH=0`; window-title 13 failures) — then the T202
  sources were restored. The cause is the box being in use: a fullscreen
  Unreal title (`UnrealWindow`) holds the foreground with `GameInputSvc`
  running, the documented input-wedge state, which is also exactly what
  **T207** was filed to fix.
