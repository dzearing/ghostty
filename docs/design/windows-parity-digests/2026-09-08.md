## Commentary

### Yesterday

Twenty-four tasks closed, and almost all of them were about **telling you the
truth about your own machine**. Signing out of your Ghoztty account used to
leave the computer you were sitting at still enrolled — three separate fixes
now take the machine with you, finish the job once the relay answers, and
un-enroll it even if you never open Ghoztty on that box again. A remote
machine running the wrong version used to look simply unreachable; it now says
so. A Ghoztty older than its own background process says so too. And the
restart that ends your sessions now explains what you get for it, instead of
just asking.

The other half of the day was the **viewer panes**. Every button in a viewer's
nav bar now names itself on hover, the feedback composer's two buttons say what
they do and answer the keyboard, and text composed with an input method
(Japanese, Chinese, anything with a candidate window) no longer arrives wearing
a quote's formatting. Small things individually; together they are the
difference between a pane that feels finished and one that feels like a
prototype.

Underneath, two robustness fixes worth naming: a full `C:` drive no longer
turns every build into a bare `error: Unexpected`, and a half-unpacked
dependency now heals itself instead of taking the box down.

### Reflection

**Two consecutive days of work are now behind the same wall.** Yesterday's
reflection called the publish cadence the weakest link; today it is the same
link, one day longer. The mechanism is sound — the request path exists, the
health line degrades, the digest names the strandees — but every one of those
is a *report* about a release that did not happen. The idea already on the
ledger ("publish on the day's last good commit, not a clock") is the shape of
the fix: the clock is the wrong trigger for a loop whose day does not end at a
fixed hour. Worth promoting from an idea to a task soon; three mornings of the
same paragraph is the signal.

**The closing rate is holding at ~24 a day and the M1 number is moving.** 277 of
344 closed, up from 266 of 338 yesterday: eleven closures against six additions
to the milestone. That is the first time in a week the denominator grew slower
than the numerator, which is what convergence actually looks like.

**The work is drifting from mechanism to manners.** Six of yesterday's closures
were not about capability at all — they were about a control saying what it
does, an error naming the real cause, a restart explaining its cost. That is
the right drift for a build approaching a cutover, and it is worth noticing
that the tasks producing it were all filed as P2 polish. The polish band is
where the perceived quality lives.

## Feature ideas

### User-facing UX

- **Paste that shows you what you are about to paste** (High) — a multi-line or
  suspiciously long clipboard drops a small preview card before it hits the
  shell, with the line count and any newline warning. The single most common
  way a terminal ruins someone's afternoon.
- **A pane that tells you when it stopped needing you** (High) — mark a pane as
  "waiting on me"; the tab and taskbar badge clear themselves the moment the
  prompt comes back. Different from a finish toast: this is about *not* having
  to check.
- **Reopen the file I just saw scroll past** (High) — recent-paths tray built
  from what the pane printed, one click to open it in a viewer pane. Terminal
  output is full of paths nobody can click twice.
- **Session names you did not have to invent** (Medium) — a persistent session
  gets an automatic name from its directory and the command it is running, so
  the chooser reads like a list of jobs rather than a list of ids.
- **Dim the panes that are not yours right now** (Medium) — unfocused panes
  lose a few points of contrast so the eye lands on the live one. A one-line
  setting, and the whole window stops competing for attention.

### Technical / stability

- **A release the loop cannot forget to cut** (High) — promote the standing
  publish-cadence idea into real work: trigger on the day's last good commit,
  with the supervisor as the backstop, so three days of stranded work stops
  being a thing a digest reports.
- **Tell me the gap in the app, not the digest** (Medium) — the About card
  already knows the installed version; have it name how many commits and which
  headline fixes are waiting in a newer release.
- **A harness that has never gone red is not a harness** (Medium) — extend
  `gate-negatives.ps1`'s demonstration rule to acceptance scripts, not just the
  two gates, so a script that cannot fail is caught at authoring time.
